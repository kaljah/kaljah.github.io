### Context
In the oil and gas industry, **liquid unloading** is a process used to remove accumulated fluids from a well to allow gas production to continue[1]. The *Compendium* distinguishes between different methods of unloading, such as:

*   **Non-plunger lift well unloading:** Typically a manual process where an operator diverts gas to an atmospheric tank (blowdown tank), reducing pressure and allowing the well's flow to push liquids out[1][2]. The gas entrained in the liquid is then vented to the atmosphere[1][2].
*   **Plunger lift well unloading:** A method that can be performed manually or in an automated mode to assist in removing liquids[1][2].

The *API Compendium* serves as a standard resource for industry operators to consistently estimate and report direct greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions across the value chain[3][4].
