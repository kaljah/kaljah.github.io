In the **API Compendium of Greenhouse Gas Emissions Methodologies for the Oil and Natural Gas Industry** (specifically the 2021 edition), **Table 6-5** and **Table 6-6** provide methane emission factors related to well completions[1].

*   **Table 6-5:** Often referenced in the context of **Well Completions with Hydraulic Fracturing** (specifically for oil and gas exploration)[1].
*   **Table 6-6:** Typically provides methane emission factors for **Onshore Well Completions without Hydraulic Fracturing**[1].

### Context and Usage
*   **Hydraulic Fracturing vs. Conventional:** The Compendium distinguishes between these two processes because completions involving hydraulic fracturing—which require high-rate flowback to clear the wellbore—generally result in a higher volume of methane emissions compared to conventional completions that do not involve hydraulic fracturing[2][3][4].
*   **Methodology:** These tables are used by industry professionals to quantify greenhouse gas emissions[5][6]. When "Reduced Emissions Completions" (RECs) are employed, the emission factors account for the capture of gas that would otherwise be vented or flared during the flowback period[7][8].
*   **Reporting:** The API Compendium serves as a foundational resource for companies to estimate and report their GHG emissions, often supporting regulatory requirements for transparency and environmental compliance[5][6].

*Note: As editions of the API Compendium are updated (e.g., 2009 vs. 2021), the specific contents of tables may be revised or renumbered[9][6]. Always verify the specific edition of the document you are referencing for your calculations[10][11].*
