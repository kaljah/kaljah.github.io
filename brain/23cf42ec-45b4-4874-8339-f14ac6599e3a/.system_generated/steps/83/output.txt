In the *Compendium of Greenhouse Gas Emissions Methodologies for the Natural Gas and Oil Industry* (published by the American Petroleum Institute), **Section 6.2.3: Well Completions** addresses the emissions associated with the final stages of well drilling[1][2].

Key details regarding this section include:

*   **Definition:** Well completions are identified as the final step of the well-drilling process[1]. During this phase, the wellbore and the reservoir near the well must be cleaned[1].
*   **Process:** Cleaning is typically achieved by producing the well so that sand, cuttings, and other reservoir fluids can be collected for recycling or disposal[1]. 
*   **Purpose:** This step is also used to evaluate the well's production rate, which allows operators to properly size production equipment[1]. 
*   **Emissions Context:** The *Compendium* provides standardized methodologies for estimating greenhouse gas emissions throughout the natural gas and oil value chain[2][3]. This document is frequently used by industry and referenced by regulators globally (including the U.S. EPA and others) to support consistent emissions reporting[4][5].
