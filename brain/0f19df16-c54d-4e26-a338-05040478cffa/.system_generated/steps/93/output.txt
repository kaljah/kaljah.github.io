The **API Compendium of Greenhouse Gas Emissions Estimation Methodologies for the Oil and Natural Gas Industry** (API Compendium) is a widely used industry resource for estimating greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, including those from **mud degassing**[1][2].

### Key Contextual Information

*   **Mud Degassing:** This process involves the removal of dissolved gases (such as methane and CO₂) from drilling fluids (mud) as they circulate back to the surface during the drilling of oil and gas wells[3]. The API Compendium provides methodologies and emission factors to help operators estimate these vented emissions[4][5].
*   **"Table 6-2":** In various editions of the API Compendium, **Table 6-2** is commonly referenced in the context of **fugitive emission factors**[6][7][8]. Users often consult this table to find default values for facility-level average fugitive emissions when site-specific data (such as exact CO₂ or CH₄ concentrations) is unavailable or when adjusting default emission estimates[7][8]. 
*   **CO₂ and Concentration:** When estimating emissions from sources like mud degassing or fugitive leaks, the API Compendium often utilizes stoichiometry or representative gas composition data (mole % of CO₂ and CH₄) to calculate mass-based emissions[9][10][8]. If the actual concentration of CO₂ or CH₄ in the gas stream differs from the default values provided in the Compendium, operators are typically directed to use site-specific analytical data for more accurate reporting[7][8].

### Usage Note
Because the API Compendium has been updated over the years (e.g., 2001, 2009, 2021 versions), the specific contents and numbering of tables can vary[1][11][12][2]. If you are working on a specific emissions report or compliance document, ensure you are referencing the version of the API Compendium specified by your regulatory or industry reporting guidelines (e.g., EPA Subpart W or other GHG protocols)[13][14][15].
