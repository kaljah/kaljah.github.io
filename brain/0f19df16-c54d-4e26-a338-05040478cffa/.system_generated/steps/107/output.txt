The **API Compendium of Greenhouse Gas Emissions Estimation Methodologies for the Oil and Natural Gas Industry** (often referred to as the **API Compendium**) provides standardized methodologies and emission factors for various industry activities[1][2].

### Mud Degassing and Drilling
In the context of drilling, **mud degassing** is a recognized source of vented methane emissions[3][4][5].
*   **Methodology:** The API Compendium includes specific procedures for estimating these emissions, typically found in sections discussing venting and exploration activities[2][6][4].
*   **Emission Factors:** The Compendium provides default emission factors for mud degassing[7]. Notably, the 2021 edition adjusted earlier values to better reflect modern onshore drilling practices, such as borehole size and porosity[5].
*   **Table References:** **Table 6-2** in several versions of the API Compendium is specifically titled or noted to contain **"Mud Degassing Vented Methane Emission Factors for Oil and Natural Gas Exploration"**[8][2][9].

### Uncertainty
The API Compendium emphasizes the importance of understanding data quality and uncertainty in emission inventories[1][10]:
*   **Uncertainty Guidance:** The document generally provides uncertainty values for emission factors where they are available[11][12].
*   **Supplementary Resources:** API, in collaboration with IPIECA, has developed separate **uncertainty guidelines** that provide detailed methods for calculating the uncertainty of various emission sources within the oil and gas sector[11][12].
*   **Data Quality:** Users are encouraged to select methodologies based on data availability and the significance of the emission source, with the Compendium providing decision trees to assist in this selection[10].
